Course Syllabus & Learning Modules

Select any module below to explore topics covered in Lecturing and Exam Preparation sessions. Topics can also be customized to match your school's current chapter.

  • 1. States of Matter, Atoms & Bonding
  • 2. Stoichiometry & The Mole Concept
  • 3. Electrochemistry & Chemical Energetics
  • 4. Chemical Reactions, Rates & Equilibria
  • 5. Acids, Bases, Salts & The Periodic Table
  • 6. Metals & Chemistry of the Environment
  • 7. Organic Chemistry
  • 8. Experimental Techniques & Paper 6

1. States of Matter, Atoms & Bonding

Visualize particle behavior, electronic configurations, and structure-property relationships.

  1. Heating/cooling curves, diffusion rates, and kinetic particle theory
  2. Isotopes, relative atomic mass, and electron shell configurations
  3. Ionic, covalent (dot-and-cross), and metallic bonding
  4. Giant covalent lattices (diamond, graphite, silicon(IV) oxide) vs simple molecular structures

2. Stoichiometry & The Mole Concept

Master quantitative chemical calculations with zero unit errors.

  1. Balancing chemical and ionic equations
  2. Moles from mass, molar gas volume (24 dm³ at r.t.p.), and solution concentration (mol/dm³)
  3. Limiting reactants, percentage yield, and percentage purity
  4. Empirical and molecular formula determination

3. Electrochemistry & Chemical Energetics

Predict electrode products and compute enthalpy changes using bond energies.

  1. Electrolysis of molten and aqueous electrolytes (conc. NaCl, CuSO₄, H₂SO₄)
  2. Electroplating, metal purification, and hydrogen-oxygen fuel cells
  3. Exothermic vs endothermic reaction pathway diagrams
  4. Bond breaking vs bond making enthalpy calculations (ΔH)

4. Chemical Reactions, Rates & Equilibria

Apply collision theory and Le Chatelier's principle to industrial syntheses.

  1. Physical vs chemical changes and collision theory (concentration, surface area, temperature, catalysts)
  2. Reversible reactions and hydrated salts (CuSO₄·5H₂O, CoCl₂)
  3. Haber process (ammonia) and Contact process (sulfuric acid) equilibrium conditions
  4. Redox reactions in terms of electron transfer and oxidation numbers

5. Acids, Bases, Salts & The Periodic Table

Master salt preparation workflows and Group 1, Group 7, and Transition Element trends.

  1. Strong vs weak acids/bases, pH scale, and oxides (acidic, basic, amphoteric, neutral)
  2. Salt preparation: titration, excess insoluble base/metal, and precipitation
  3. Group I alkali metals, Group VII halogens (displacement), and transition metal catalysts/colors

6. Metals & Chemistry of the Environment

Connect the reactivity series with metal extraction, rust prevention, and climate chemistry.

  1. Reactivity series, displacement reactions, and thermal decomposition
  2. Blast furnace extraction of iron and Hall-Héroult extraction of aluminum
  3. Rusting prevention (sacrificial protection, galvanizing) and alloys
  4. Water purification, NPK fertilizers, air pollutants, and greenhouse effect

7. Organic Chemistry

Understand homologous series, isomerism, cracking, and addition/condensation polymers.

  1. Fractional distillation of petroleum, alkanes (substitution), and alkenes (addition with Br₂/steam)
  2. Alcohols (fermentation vs hydration of ethene) and carboxylic acids/esters
  3. Addition polymerization (poly(ethene)) and condensation polymers (nylon, PET)

8. Experimental Techniques & Paper 6

Nail chromatography, titration reading, and cation/anion/gas qualitative tests.

  1. Rf values, crystallization, fractional distillation, and titration apparatus accuracy
  2. Flame tests, NaOH/NH₃ precipitate tests for cations, halide/sulfate/carbonate tests
  3. Planning investigation questions in Paper 6
