Cambridge International AS & A-Level Chemistry (9701)

Taught by Torche Education's Chemical Engineering specialists, this course breaks down Cambridge AS & A-Level Chemistry (9701) into crystal-clear physical principles, inorganic patterns, organic reaction mechanisms, and spectroscopic analysis (IR, Mass Spec, ¹³C and ¹H NMR).

Course Syllabus & Learning Modules

Select any module below to explore topics covered in Lecturing and Exam Preparation sessions. Topics can also be customized to match your school's current chapter.

  • 1. Atomic Structure, Stoichiometry & Bonding
  • 2. States of Matter, Energetics & Electrochemistry
  • 3. Equilibria, Kinetics & Catalysis
  • 4. Inorganic Chemistry (Period 3, Group 2, Group 17 & Transition Metals)
  • 5. AS Organic Chemistry
  • 6. A2 Organic Chemistry (Arenes, Nitrogen & Synthesis)
  • 7. Analytical Techniques (IR, Mass Spec & NMR)
  • 8. Paper 3 & Paper 5 Mastery

1. Atomic Structure, Stoichiometry & Bonding

Build precision with orbitals, ionization energy trends, VSEPR shapes, and intermolecular forces.

  1. s, p, d atomic orbitals, electron configurations, and successive ionization energies
  2. Mass spectrometry, back-titration, and gas stoichiometry calculations
  3. Hybridization (sp, sp², sp³), VSEPR shapes, bond angles, and hydrogen bonding

2. States of Matter, Energetics & Electrochemistry

Master Hess's law, Born-Haber cycles, Gibbs free energy, and electrode potentials.

  1. Ideal gas equation, lattice energy, and Born-Haber cycle construction
  2. Enthalpy of solution/hydration, entropy ΔS, and Gibbs free energy ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  3. Standard electrode potentials (E°), Nernst equation, feasibility, and Faraday electrolysis (Q = It)

3. Equilibria, Kinetics & Catalysis

Solve quantitative Kc, Kp, Ka, pH buffer, Ksp, and rate-equation problems.

  1. Kc, Kp, and Le Chatelier's principle in industrial equilibria
  2. Brønsted-Lowry acids/bases, pH of weak acids, buffer calculations, and titration curves
  3. Solubility product (Ksp) and common-ion effect; partition coefficient (Kpc)
  4. Orders of reaction, rate constants, half-life, Arrhenius/Boltzmann curves, and homogeneous/heterogeneous catalysis

4. Inorganic Chemistry (Period 3, Group 2, Group 17 & Transition Metals)

Explain periodicity, thermal stability, halide reactions, and ligand-exchange colors.

  1. Period 3 oxides & chlorides reactions with water and acid/base trends
  2. Group 2 nitrate/carbonate thermal stability and sulfate/hydroxide solubility
  3. Group 17 oxidizing power, disproportionation, and halide-sulfuric acid redox
  4. Transition metals: d-orbital splitting, ligand exchange, stability constants (Kstab), and catalysis

5. AS Organic Chemistry

Master nomenclature, stereoisomerism, and nucleophilic/electrophilic mechanisms.

  1. Structural, cis-trans, and optical isomerism (chiral centers)
  2. Free-radical substitution (alkanes) and electrophilic addition (alkenes, Markovnikov rule)
  3. SN1 vs SN2 nucleophilic substitution in halogenoalkanes
  4. Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, and functional group tests

6. A2 Organic Chemistry (Arenes, Nitrogen & Synthesis)

Synthesize multi-step organic routes involving benzene, amines, amides, and polymers.

  1. Electrophilic substitution in benzene (nitration, Friedel-Crafts, directing effects of -OH/-NH₂/-NO₂)
  2. Phenol acidity, acyl chlorides, phenylamines, and azo dye diazonium coupling
  3. Amino acids (zwitterions), electrophoresis, condensation polymers, and degradable polyesters
  4. Multi-step synthetic route planning maps

7. Analytical Techniques (IR, Mass Spec & NMR)

Deduce unknown molecular structures confidently from combined spectra.

  1. Infrared (IR) absorption bands and mass spectrometry (M+1, M+2 peaks & fragmentation)
  2. ¹³C NMR chemical shifts and symmetry counting
  3. ¹H (proton) NMR: chemical shifts, integration ratios, n+1 splitting patterns, and D₂O shake

8. Paper 3 & Paper 5 Mastery

Excel in volumetric/enthalpy practical analysis, planning, and uncertainty evaluation.

  1. Titration concordancy, thermometric extrapolations, and qualitative salt tests
  2. Paper 5 experimental design, controlled variables, graph gradients, and anomalous data critique
